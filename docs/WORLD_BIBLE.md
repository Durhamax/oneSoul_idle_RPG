# One Soul — World Bible

**Status:** Living document — v0.1 (narrative north star)
**Last updated:** 2026-11-09
**Purpose:** The single source of truth for the world, story, theme, and tone of *One Soul*. Every region, mission, item, enemy, and art decision should be answerable against this document. If something on screen doesn't serve one of these ideas, it's decoration — and in this game, decoration has to earn its place.

> This is a narrative/experience bible, not a systems spec. For mechanics see `GAME_CONTEXT.md`, `OneSoul_Crafting_System_Design.md`, and `EQUIPMENT_SYSTEM_GUIDE.md`.

## 0. The North Star (read this first)

**One Soul is a game you play with half your attention that still delivers the full-attention feeling of a world opening up — because every number, unlock, and screen is dressed as a place, a mood, and a consequence.**

It is an idle RPG about **the one soul willing to keep looking at a truth everyone else refuses to see** — set on an alien-touched world where a marginalized people's righteous war is a tragic distraction from a reckoning none of them can even perceive.

### The three player pillars (the promise)
1. **Second-screen native.** Nothing in the core loop requires the player's eyes. Every meaningful state change survives being noticed 20 seconds late and still feels good. Mobile-ready is a discipline accepted now, not a port bolted on later.
2. **Wonder through unlocks.** You can't walk over a hill and *see* the next place — so the **unlock is the hill.** Locked content is visible-but-mysterious (silhouettes, "???", fog), and crossing a threshold reveals it. That reveal is the dopamine and the wonder.
3. **Humane completionism.** The 1,000-hour RPG completion dream, delivered to people who can't give 1,000 active hours. Idle time counts toward the same deep completion a hardcore grinder earns. The map filling in *is* the trophy wall.

### The one law that protects the pillars: no dead hexes
Dead time is the quit trigger. **Every region must always offer at least one meaningful idle action to the player who just arrived** — and the universal fallback is Navigation itself: even a region too dangerous to gather or fight in can always be *explored* (train Navigation, raise discovery, resolve silhouettes). The worst-case hex is never dead; it's "the place where I grind the unknown into the known."

## 1. The Thematic Spine

**Every person in this story is defined by their relationship to a truth they cannot see.**

The alien threat is literally imperceptible to humans (*beyond human metrology*). So the whole saga is about **who looks, who looks away, and what it costs:**

- **Elara** *felt* the invisible and was destroyed by it — not because she was special, but because she was the one awake. The truth fell to her by chance, unprovable, so even she read it as madness.
- **Unity** refuses to look. They edit the fear out of their lives and call an alien beacon a "Cradle." Denial as a way of life.
- **The Elarans** looked once, in fear — then forgot they ever had. The knowledge decayed into appetite. Forgetting as a way of life.
- **The player** is the one soul who *won't stop looking.*

### What "One Soul" means
Not a unique being, not a chosen one. **In a world of millions looking away, one soul is willing to carry the weight of seeing.** The title is about *moral and perceptual solitude*, not specialness. This also solves the idle-RPG lone-hero problem: the player becomes the one the world rests on **because they are the only one still facing it**, and becomes *strong* because they are willing to be *changed* by what they see — to pick up the tools, taboos, and powers everyone else drops for comfort or principle.

### The fusion of mechanics and meaning
**Progression is transgression.** Unity forbids weapons and advanced tech. To survive, the player climbs the crafting tree anyway — firearms, machines, exotic Seed-derived technology. So the power curve is not a number tree; it is **the Covenant breaking in one pair of hands.** Every tier the player climbs is another principle they burn because the world took too much from them to stay gentle. The crafting ascent is the protagonist's moral arc, lived through the player's own actions.

## 2. Cosmology — The Asterion and the Lumen Seeds

- Long before humanity, an ancient alien civilization — the **Asterion** — seeded the Milky Way with millions of engineered meteoroids, each carrying a **Lumen Seed**: an exotic power source refined from harvested atomic matter in Dyson-like star-plants near the galactic core.
- A Lumen Seed is **not a weapon** (though it *could* be weaponized; its arrival is an asteroid-level impact). Its true purpose is a **colonization beacon** — exotic matter engineered near black-hole event horizons and flung across the galaxy to evaluate a world's stability over aeons.
- A Seed **never turns on or off.** Its exotic lattice continuously emits decay signatures as an inherent physical property. These emissions are **undetectable by human science** — humans don't measure the required exotic frequencies.
- The Asterion live beside the galaxy's central supermassive black hole, where **extreme time dilation** compresses the Seed's multi-million-year decay profile: what unfolds over *millions of years* mid-galaxy reads to Asterion sensors as only *a few generations*. Relativistic blue-shift makes the Seeds shine as crisp, trackable beacons against galactic noise.
- A Seed that stays stable — unbroken by volcanism, tectonics, or catastrophe — tells the Asterion: **"this world is viable for settlement."**
- **The Asterion are coming.** A fleet has been crossing from the galactic core for hundreds of thousands of years toward the beacon they planted and know is calling. They cannot be perceived by humans. They will arrive *centuries after* humanity did.

> **Thematic note:** An idle game is a game about time passing while you're away — eons compressed into glances. The antagonist's entire nature (deep time, imperceptibility, patience) is the shape of the genre itself. The fiction and the core loop are the same gesture.

## 3. Aurora Vitae and the Two Seeds

Two Lumen Seeds struck the exoplanet **Aurora Vitae** ("dawn of life").

- **Alpha — Infernus.** Corrupted, and the **first Seed anyone ever located** (hence "Alpha" in the Elaran catalog). It crashed into volcanic badlands; magma intrusion damaged most of its isotopic lattice, leaving a weak, broken signal. Elaran prospectors discovered the remnant by accident in **895 AL** and catalogued it as **Asterion Isotope AV-S1** — presumed alien, origin unknown, emitting energy *beyond human metrology.* They do not grasp its purpose; they only know it is power. The located source is designated **Site Alpha** (hex 80).
- **Beta — The Cradle.** Intact, and still signaling continuously for millions of years. It subtly transforms the land: enhanced soil metabolism, hyperdense lush forest, resilient flora, long-term biome stability. The crater it formed became a 100-mile bowl of impossible abundance, and its viability is *why* Aurora Vitae reads as habitable at all. Studying Alpha, the Elarans **detected a second, matching signature** and designated its source **Site Beta** — but they have only tracked it, not reached it. Tracing it leads straight into Unity's last refuge. **Unity live directly on top of it, utterly unaware:** they run no deepground mining to stumble on it and lack the technology to detect the signature, so they simply receive its gift — the Cradle's abundance — **blissfully ignorant until the intrusion.** (So the invasion isn't a land grab; it's the Elarans *following Site Beta's signature home.*)

The planet has two poles of meaning: **the green pole (Cradle / life / the intact Seed)** and **the fire pole (Infernus / corruption / the broken Seed).** The player's journey runs between them.

> **The Cradle's double meaning (late-game reveal):** Unity named it "the Cradle" for the cradle-of-life abundance they found. It is a cradle — *for the Asterion's arrival.* The home was always the trap. ("The Scar," an old name for the impact site, is **deprecated.**)

## 4. Human History

### The diaspora
Earth is left desolate by overpopulation and overconsumption. Humanity scatters **multiple arks to multiple imaged candidate worlds** to hedge survival. One ark is sent to Aurora Vitae — chosen the mundane way, by long-range imaging that flagged a breathable atmosphere. (That atmosphere is breathable *because of the Beta Seed.* Nobody knows this. No one was lured; they just saw green and went.) **This opens a much larger human diaspora for future story.**

### The Covenant of Unity
The pioneers are indoctrinated to the **Covenant of Unity** — an anchor against the lessons of Earth's mismanagement. It emphasizes integration with nature, conservative and fair use of resources, and places **very little emphasis on defense.**

### Elara Voss and the waking schism
- Mid-voyage, a passenger named **Elara Voss** wakes from cryo-sleep centuries too early, alone. She begins to perceive distant **orbs** — the Asterion fleet itself, crossing toward the same world, which **detects the inbound human ark and nudges it off its path of incidence** to keep their test-world clean of a contaminating variable arriving ahead of them. (At that range and relative velocity they can only *nudge* — the humans land anyway.)
- Elara **physically feels the ship shift** and knows something is out there. But she can't prove it, thinks she's dreaming, and goes mad. Her navigation-log entries degrade from concise and logical into rambling nonsense, spilling onto desk panels and the walls of the halls. She chooses a hidden place to die where no one will find her (**location unwritten**). Before the end she even bypasses the AI to edit the Covenant to warn: *we are not alone.*
- Thousands of pioneers wake at arrival to **Elara's writings and no proof.** Everyone reads the same text. **They split on how to read it** — some panic, some dismiss it as solitude-driven paranoia. **The division is an act of interpretation, not information.** This is the founding schism, and it states the theme in the first moment: a truth you can't verify, and a people deciding what to do with the discomfort of not knowing.

### Timeline anchor
Calendar is **AL** (after landing/arrival). Arrival ≈ 0 AL. Factions diverge over the following centuries. **895 AL:** Elaran prospectors find Alpha (AV-S1) in Infernus. Present day: somewhat after.

## 5. The Factions

### Unity (the player's people)
A small, grounded, intelligent people who stayed true to the Covenant: live with the land, take only what's needed, never repeat Earth. Over centuries they have been **pushed into the Cradle and a small cluster of adjacent territories — a tiny fraction of Aurora Vitae** — grown in their own silo. They have been treated by the Elarans the way a land-rooted indigenous people gets marginalized by an expansionist, resource-extracting power: as backward, useless, in the way.

> **Handling note:** This marginalized-people / expansionist-power dynamic, and the protagonist's radicalization into violence in response to it, is a **core pillar — resonant and powerful, and only an asset if handled with weight and specificity, never as set-dressing.** Keep it in view as a theme, not a backdrop.

### The Elarans (the enemy — and the shield)
Named for Elara, they are the larger population who took her warning seriously. The Covenant's abandonment **began in fear, not greed** — and the belief never fully died; it survives, concentrated, in a **bi-modal society:**

- **The elite / militarized class still genuinely believe the threat** and drive an existential war economy of intense resource burn. They read Infernus's AV-S1 signal as confirmation and prepare for an enemy they can prove to no one below them. *Not cartoon conquerors — true believers doing a monstrous thing for a real reason.* (This is the motive the war economy requires.)
- **The population mass** lives in a managed, consumerist near-idiocracy — laboring to power the burn without the *why.* For them the original terror has faded to background myth; they are Elaran by nation, not by knowledge.

Between the two modes sits **the Threshold:** potential is evaluated in childhood; the few who clear it continue through education into the informed/elite strata, the rest are dropped into the labor force young. A ruthless meritocratic sort that emerged on a new world with few rules and one fear — the mirror image of Unity's answer to the same Earth-trauma. **Two children of one catastrophe: Unity chose gentleness, the Elarans chose preparation.** The thing that made the Elarans humanity's only possible shield is a society built in the image of the overconsumption the Covenant fled — and it may be the only reason anyone survives.

> **The Elarans can never be cartoon villains.** The entire ending depends on the player — and the audience — being able to see them as human. (See §8.)

## 6. The Protagonist — the One Soul

A member of Unity, intimately aware of how their people have been wronged, and **driven by vengeance and hatred.** When the Elaran drones breach the Cradle — the last corner Unity has left — the whole faction erupts in rage and fights to defend it. **The player is the one who doesn't stop there.** Unlike the rest of Unity, who hold to the Covenant even unto extinction, **this one will not stay peaceful past the breaking point** — they pick up the violence their culture forbids, and they follow the clues and intrigue of the invasion *outward*, away from the Cradle, chasing the question nobody else will: *why?*

- **The curiosity is a weapon, not detachment.** The player asks "why" the way an avenger does — *I need to understand my enemy to destroy them* — and the tragedy is that understanding corrodes the hatred it was meant to serve. Every clue that makes the Elarans comprehensible makes them human. That internal war — a partisan who can't stop interrogating his own cause — *is* the character.
- **His anger is both earned and blinding.** Unity really were wronged. And that legitimate hatred is also exactly the kind of human division that leaves humanity split while the real reckoning closes in. He is the sharpest possible vessel for the theme.

## 7. The Voices (lore delivery through two registers)

No chatty travel companion — that would puncture the one-soul solitude. Instead, **two voices that never overlap:**

### The living voice — the Elaran guide
A weathered **Elaran outcast** (exiled, or simply done with his people's greed) who haunts the wild margins the player keeps crossing. **Not a companion** — a recurring, grudging transaction. The player *needs him to survive beyond the Cradle, and hates that he does.* He trades hard survival knowledge and scraps about what the Elarans are building; he never offers warmth. He does not share the player's curiosity — he's past caring *why*; he just knows *how to live*. He is also a quiet **mirror/warning:** a man who questioned his side until he had no side left, and hollowed out — what the player risks becoming.

Why he's the right choice:
- He makes the ending **inevitable, not surprising** — the player's whole survival is already built on one Elaran's help; the finale just scales that to all of them.
- He **humanizes the enemy structurally**, through dependence, not a lecture.
- He externalizes the protagonist's contradiction in every interaction.

Lean him **as close to "the enemy" as the player can bear.** The discomfort is the feature. (Fallback if a full Elaran is too far: a Unity-born man who went to live the Elaran way and belongs to neither — but every step *toward* the enemy strengthens the theme.)

### The dead voice — Elara's log
**Found, not spoken.** Her writings are foundational myth that *both* factions already know — so the player is **not uncovering the story; everyone knows the story.** The player uncovers **proof** that the madwoman was *right.* Her words never change — the *ground under them* does. As the player digs up physical evidence (the Seeds, Asterion traces, Elaran construction), her "ramblings" retroactively become lucid prophecy. **The reversal is engineered through gameplay, not cutscene.**

> **The bookend:** Open the game on a wordless, cryptic prologue — the ship, the drifting orbs, a hand scribbling in the dark — shown once and never explained. Three movements later, the player realizes they watched the answer in the first thirty seconds and couldn't see it either. The original Ark / nav log can be a *known monument* (likely in Unity's capital) that the player returns to and reads one final time, now understanding every line.

## 8. The Plot — Three Movements

### Movement I — The Garden and the Question *(The Cradle, outward)*
Tone: warm, over-saturated, uncanny abundance — the beacon's glow dressed as paradise. The drone breach shatters it. The player, a vengeful scout, gives chase and is lured off course by clues nobody else stops to notice. **Emotional key:** curiosity and fury piercing comfort. As the map opens from safety, palettes cool and complicate.

### Movement II — The Homecoming and the Rot *(Lost Unity homelands → Elaran lands)*
Tone: memory and grief curdling into violence. The player walks back through the overgrown ruins of homes Unity was driven from, reading their own people's past, then into Elaran country, where the enemy becomes *human* — brutal, yes, but also afraid, also forgetting, also (unknowingly) right. Here **progression is transgression:** to get this far the player has taken up everything Unity forbade, out of grief. **Emotional key:** the enemy becomes human; the self becomes the enemy. Palette shifts from green to industry and fire; the tech-tier visuals ramp.

### Movement III — The Reckoning *(The truth, the return)*
Tone: cosmic dread, then forced unity. The Seeds revealed, Elara vindicated, and for the first time in the whole game **the invisible becomes visible** — the orbs she felt, the fleet that nudged the ship, appear in a sky that changes color. The Cradle's name flips meaning. **Emotional key:** the entire human war recontextualized as a tragic sideshow; salvation demands that enemies become one.

### The ending (open for follow-up)
The game ends on **the turn, not the war.** The **Elarans — the enemy the player hunted and killed across the whole game — form a line between the Cradle and the sky, ready to protect it from the Asterion arrival.** Salvation arrives wearing the face of everyone the player killed; his righteous fury was aimed at humanity's shield. It is "sweet" in the cruelest, best way, and it holds on a breath. **The actual Asterion war is the sequel.**

### Recurring motifs
- **The invisible made visible.** The threat is unseen for the entire game; the climax is the first time anyone *sees* it. This rhymes with the core discovery mechanic — every locked region glimpsed as a silhouette is a small echo of the one great shadow behind the world. The loop and the theme are the same gesture.
- **Elara's reversal.** The same words, re-meant by what the player has learned.
- **The Cradle double meaning.** Named for life; a cradle for *them.*

## 9. The Logical Spine (canon integrity)

The causal chain, with the two load-bearing plot holes plugged:

1. Beta Seed terraforms Aurora Vitae → habitable atmosphere.
2. Earth dies; humanity scatters arks to imaged candidate worlds; one comes here (never knowing *why* it's green).
3. **[Plot hole #1 — plugged]** The Asterion fleet, closing on the same world, nudges the human ark off course to keep their test-world clean of a contaminating variable arriving ahead of them. They can only nudge; humans land. *This also pre-loads Act 2's stakes: to the Asterion, humans were never a people — just a smudge on the lens.*
4. Elara alone perceives it, can't prove it, breaks, hides, dies, leaves warnings.
5. The pioneers wake, read the same text, and **split on interpretation** — the founding schism.
6. Over centuries: Unity denies and is pushed into the Cradle; Elarans' fear decays into greed and militarized expansion; in 895 AL they find Alpha.
7. **[Plot hole #2 — plugged]** The truth was **never hidden** — everyone knows Elara's story. So the player doesn't recover the *story*; the player recovers **proof.** The evidence trail (Seeds, Asterion traces, Elaran construction) is what re-means a diary the world already dismissed.
8. Elaran drones hunt Infernus-matching signals to the Cradle (the strongest Seed of all) → invasion → the player follows the question outward → assembles the proof → the Asterion arrive.

**No coincidence is load-bearing except the one that should be — which ordinary soul happened to be awake — and that coincidence is the theme.**

## 10. Art & Experience Direction

The game is a UI, so the UI must do the work of placing the player in a world. Two axes, always on:

- **Mood-per-region.** The background, color grade, and particles *are* the place. Arctic goes white and cold with drifting snow; Infernus burns; the Cradle is golden-green and almost *too* alive (the beacon, subliminally wrong). Travel is rewarded with a full re-skin of the Gather and Combat UIs to the current region's data. **The art is the payoff of Navigation, not decoration on top of it.**
- **Mood-per-era.** Early game feels primal and small (warm campfire light, earthy palettes, hand-forged). Late game hums with power (cold blues, energy particles, a more advanced UI). The player should *feel how far they've come* just by how the screen looks. The fantasy→Seed-tech ascent is visible in the chrome itself.

Principles:
- **Diegetic over decorative.** A particle layer that shifts with where you are and what you're doing is a world; an always-on ambient one is wallpaper.
- **Silhouettes everywhere.** Show the shadows of what hasn't been unlocked. Wonder is the engine; the unseen is the point.
- **The Cradle should feel uncanny.** Too saturated, too fertile — beautiful and subliminally wrong, foreshadowing the whole game without a word.
- **Withhold the Asterion.** Shadows, signals, and silhouettes until the very end. The one image the game never shows until Movement III is the thing Elara felt.

## 11. Content Method — how to author a region without a blank-page crisis

Every region is **two columns stacked** (the method already proven in the Tier 1 table):

1. A **Progression beat** — what system/content it introduces or scales (new tools, a skill, a combat type, an elite, an incursion).
2. A **Mood + Lore beat** — what it makes the player *feel* and the *question it plants* ("serene and calm," "curious and reminiscent," "stark reminder of brutality," "what are they doing?").

**A region that only scales numbers is a dead hex. A region that pairs a number with a feeling and a question is a place.** Both columns must be filled, every time. This is what keeps a vast map from becoming a spreadsheet — and what makes authoring scalable instead of infinite.

### The variety doctrine (vast but not overwhelming)
- **Texture variety** (snow vs. lava, fox vs. wolf, copper vs. tin): cheap, huge mood payoff, near-zero cognitive load. **Make this enormous.**
- **Combinatorial variety** (same verbs, thousands of recipes/paths): the free lunch and the "solved for years" depth. Lives in the crafting graph and the optimization space where systems multiply.
- **Mechanical variety** (new rules the player must learn): expensive, a potential quit-point. Spend it like gold — a dozen or two landmarks across the whole game.

## 12. Canon Names & Glossary

**The map:** 80 hexes across 10 tiers (green pole → fire pole). The full POI layout lives in `MAP_POI.md`. Named hexes = authored story beats; numbered-only hexes = texture.

**Current / canon:**
- **Aurora Vitae** — the exoplanet.
- **The Cradle** (hex 1) — Unity's home; the ~100-mile crater (half land, half ocean) atop the intact **Beta** Seed. Tier 1.
- **Verduun** — ancestral Unity forest north of the Cradle, now Elaran-occupied (bunkers). Tier 1 hexes: Verduun Forest South / East / North.
- **Riveria** — contested river/port region between Unity's gulf and the Elaran east (Varyx = Riveria Port City; Riveria Headwaters). Tier 3. *(Current — supersedes the old-map "deprecated" note.)*
- **Liora — the Landing Site** (hex 31, Tier 5 Highlands) — where the Ark came down. Humanity's origin point on Aurora Vitae; the home of the bookend. **Nova Liora** (hex 50) is a later Elaran naval base named for it.
- **Elaria** — the Elaran capital (Elaria Metropolis / Suburbs; Bay of Elaria; Industrial District). Tier 7, "lore heavy." *(This is the Elaran heartland — "take down Elaria" from the backstory was literal.)*
- **Infernus** — volcanic badlands (Tier 10); site of the corrupted **Alpha** Seed, catalogued by Elarans as **AV-S1**. Contains Heart of Infernus (76) and **Site Alpha** (80, the Final Quest).
- **The Asterion** — the returning alien civilization. **Lumen Seed** — their colonization beacon. **Covenant of Unity** — the founding doctrine. **Elara Voss** — the witness. **AL** — the calendar.

**Deprecated (do not use — old-map names since replaced):** The Scar (→ the Cradle), The Crescent, Silvaris Range, Sere Plains, Ecanica (→ Elaria), "Nexus of Unity." Unity's capital hex is the Cradle itself (1); a distinct capital name is still open.
